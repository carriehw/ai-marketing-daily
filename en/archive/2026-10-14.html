Amex Launches Intent Intelligence Framework to Keep AI Agent Purchases in Check

Key Highlights
  • The Create Intent API pre-captures instructions — merchant, budget, preferences — and generates a verified token before any agent transaction executes.
  • Amex survey: 66% of US consumers are likely to use AI agents for price research, 61% for product recommendations.
  • The framework extends Amex's ACE Developer Toolkit launched in April 2026, with the company positioning it as an industry standard.
Industry Insight

Agentic commerce is here; brands must understand authorization protocols to position in AI-mediated purchase journeys.

Read our full write-up

American Express officially launched its 'Intent Intelligence Framework' on October 13, 2026, as the security core of its ACE (Agentic Commerce Experiences) Developer Toolkit, which was first introduced in April 2026. The initiative directly addresses the nondeterministic behavior inherent in large language models — a risk where AI agents can deviate from user instructions during task execution, such as purchasing Adidas shoes when Nike was specified.

The framework operates through a four-step mechanism. First, the Create Intent API captures the user's purchase instructions along with defined constraints — merchant name, budget ceiling, and product preferences. The user then authenticates and approves the captured intent. The system then generates a unique Intent ID and a Proof of Intent Token. Finally, at checkout, a secure digital payment credential replaces the actual card number, ensuring the real account is never exposed.

Amex VP Hannah Bozian explained that the key design principle is to 'pull intent earlier into the transaction flow,' intercepting deviations before funds move rather than managing disputes afterward. Unlike reactive chargeback mechanisms, this framework is architecturally proactive. Bozian also noted that dispute volume from agentic commerce has not yet risen noticeably — making the current rollout a preventive measure rather than a crisis response.

Amex's Trendex consumer survey found that within the next 12 months, 66% of U.S. consumers are likely to use AI agents for price research and 61% for product recommendations. This consumer appetite signals that agentic commerce is approaching rapid mainstream adoption, making pre-emptive authorization standards strategically important. American Express has stated its intent to position the framework as an industry-wide standard for AI-powered transactions, with the ACE toolkit now open to developers.

Industry Insight

Alibaba officially launched 'Qwen Go' on October 13, 2026, within its Qwen app — a personal AI agent powered by the Qwen 3.8 Agentic model. Unlike conventional AI assistants limited to answering questions or generating text, Qwen Go autonomously executes complex, real-world tasks across multiple platforms.

Officially demonstrated use cases illustrate the agent's operational range. In one flight-delay compensation scenario, the user had forgotten their own booking details — Qwen Go independently logged into the relevant platform to retrieve historical orders, contacted human customer service, and ultimately negotiated a platform's initial offer of a 70-yuan voucher into an equivalent cash payment. In an e-commerce price-protection case, the agent successfully applied for a 52-yuan refund. Other verified scenarios include completing a car maintenance booking after price-checking, reserving a badminton court based on user time preferences, and conducting shopping comparisons by factoring in subsidies, coupons, and trade-in discounts.

Industry Insight

Rising legal risk for frontier AI firms signals compliance headwinds that may slow enterprise AI partnerships.

Read our full write-up

U.S. District Judge Edward Davila of the Northern District of California ruled on October 13, 2026, ordering OpenAI to produce relevant hiring records and internal documents to Apple within 5 to 14 days of the order. The case originates from a trade secrets lawsuit Apple filed against OpenAI in July 2026.

Apple's core allegation describes a systematic pattern of behavior: multiple former Apple engineers used company-issued Apple laptops to download confidential files containing 'unannounced technologies, features, and products' before leaving to join OpenAI. Some individuals are alleged to have coached others on what materials to bring to OpenAI interviews. The case is closely tied to OpenAI's rumored development of its first consumer hardware device.

The court-ordered discovery is precisely scoped. It covers all interview materials OpenAI used when interviewing former Apple employees for roles in consumer hardware and robotics — including checklists, procedural guides, scripts, technical presentations, and any documents referencing Apple technology. The order also covers private LINE messages between OpenAI employees Chang Liu and Alyssa Peng, plus depositions of Liu, Peng, and OpenAI's chief hardware officer Tang Yew Tan — all former Apple employees. Already-collected materials must be produced within 5 days; remaining materials within 14 days.

The judge denied Apple's broader discovery requests, including expedited access to OpenAI's R&D in areas such as metal finishes and power management, and broader investigation into what former employees created at OpenAI. Apple retains the right to refile narrower requests on those topics. The case's trajectory will be among the most closely watched intellectual property disputes in the technology sector over the coming months.

Apple iOS 27 Directly Blocks The Trade Desk from Safari Ads as Programmatic Losses Mount

Since Yesterday

On Oct 13 we reported Safari's tracking block causing eCPM to fall over 10% in week one, and iOS 27 expanding blocks to hundreds of programmatic data vendors. Today's new development: The Trade Desk's cookie domain is now specifically named on the blocklist, escalating Apple's targeting from generic tracking tech to a leading DSP by name — the structural impact on agency programmatic buying is more direct than yesterday's picture suggested.

Key Highlights
  • iOS 27 added The Trade Desk's cookie domain to Safari's blocklist, directly affecting programmatic campaigns targeting Apple device users via that DSP.
  • Industry estimates show eCPM falling over 10% in the first week, with losses now extending to hundreds of additional programmatic data companies.
  • The move accelerates the cookieless transition; agencies must deploy contextual targeting and first-party data alternatives urgently to maintain audience reach.
Industry Insight

Programmatic strategies relying on The Trade Desk for iOS audiences need urgent reassessment; first-party data alternatives are now critical.

Read our full write-up

Apple released iOS 27 on September 14, 2026, triggering a significant disruption across the programmatic advertising ecosystem. The update expanded WebKit's existing policy of unconditionally blocking domains engaged in cross-site user tracking and addressable ID matching. The blocklist sweep caught not only The Trade Desk's identity solution UIDAPI.com, but also ID5, Audigent, LiveRamp, and Permutive — effectively targeting the entire independent ad tech identity layer.

The deeper problem is that Apple's block extended beyond identity infrastructure to The Trade Desk's core ad serving capabilities. Ian Meyers, The Trade Desk's Senior Director of Engineering, filed an urgent ticket on WebKit's public Bugzilla tracker (Bug #324771), explicitly stating: 'Adsrvr.org is The Trade Desk's core ad request and delivery domain, not identity.' With adsrvr.org blocked, The Trade Desk cannot bid or serve ads on Safari on iOS 27 devices at all — a far more severe consequence than losing tracking capability alone.

The episode raised pointed competitive fairness questions. Meyers included a screenshot in his bug report showing that on a yahoo.com non-private browsing session, The Trade Desk bids were blocked while Google's bids via ad.doubleclick.net passed through without interference. This asymmetry drew immediate industry scrutiny over whether Apple's enforcement selectively disadvantages independent DSPs while leaving Google's infrastructure intact on Safari. Apple's Manager of WebKit Privacy and Ad Tech, John Wilander, acknowledged the ticket, responding that he would 'let you know if and when any changes are available for you to test,' with no timeline offered.

As of AdExchanger's September 29 report, two scenarios remained in play: the block on adsrvr.org was an inadvertent error that Apple would correct upon review, or Apple was deliberately extending its blocklist from identity domains to ad delivery infrastructure itself — which would effectively shut independent DSPs out of Safari on iOS 27 devices entirely. The story broke during Advertising Week New York, with the programmatic industry described as 'buzzing madly like the inhabitants of an overturned beehive.' Neither Apple nor The Trade Desk responded to requests for comment before publication.

Agencies Race to Adopt Synthetic Audiences, Cutting Strategy Brief Timelines from 30 Days to Three

Key Highlights
  • Goodway Group partnered with Arima to run incrementality tests against simulated US, UK, and German consumer populations before real-world activation.
  • Magnum, Vans, and Kenvue used Vurvey Labs' synthetic personas, cutting strategy brief timelines from 30 days to three days.
  • TransUnion cautioned that poor-quality source data will compound errors at scale in synthetic models, urging rigorous vetting.
Industry Insight

AI-simulated research dramatically cuts pre-campaign planning time — a capability worth evaluating immediately.

Read our full write-up

Synthetic audience technology reached an inflection point in the second half of 2026, with several major agencies and brand clients moving from experimental trials to systematic deployment. The central catalyst is a dramatic compression of strategy research cycles: for Johnson & Johnson's Aveeno body care line, using Vurvey Labs' Freckle tool, a consumer strategy brief that previously took 30 days now takes three — a timeline that directly accelerated the launch of the Aveeno Sensitive Skin Scents range earlier in 2026. Unilever's Magnum and VF Corporation's Vans brand have adopted the same toolchain for product development decisions.

Goodway Group formalized a partnership with statistical modeling firm Arima in August 2026, blending US Census Bureau data, Bureau of Labor Statistics data, Nielsen data, and client first-party data to build synthetic audiences for incrementality testing and media spend simulation. Goodway is simultaneously building synthetic audience personas through Optable and is exploring extending the technology into pricing strategy and distribution channel decisions — well beyond media buying. Goodway EVP Frampton-Calero articulated the core value: 'If we spend at different levels…what would happen? Against a synthetic audience, we can simulate it.' That simulation capability proves particularly valuable in scenarios where regional or product-line research data is 'usually quite limited.'

Beauty brand Sacheu is using Limitless Labs to build virtual focus groups from TikTok and Instagram creator posts to test marketing message variants. Havas participated in Vurvey Labs' $8.5 million Series A funding round, closed November 2025. Dentsu has maintained a relationship with Evidenza for media planning for over a year. WPP is using charitable donation data to drive programmatic media buys. Jellyfish and Code & Theory are leveraging an Adobe partnership to generate creative personas from client first-party and third-party data, applying them across the full pitch-to-production pipeline.

The industry is nonetheless candid about the technology's limitations. Goodway's Reynolds warns that LLMs default to 'the top of the bell curve — the most common answer,' making them liable to miss edge-case consumer insight; Vurvey's differentiator over pure-LLM solutions is precisely its ingestion of qualitative video survey data from real consumers. Hoffman stresses that synthetic tools 'inform judgment, but don't replace judgment,' while Clark cautions that personas built on non-proprietary data will produce unreliable outputs, and flags that agentic buying carries a budget concentration risk — potentially exhausting spend in a single day. The industry consensus is shifting from whether to adopt synthetic audiences to how to build a reliable enough data foundation to make them trustworthy.

ByteDance Debuts World Model: Text Prompts Generate Explorable 3D Interactive Environments

Key Highlights
  • World models create persistent, explorable digital spaces with physics simulation and object interaction, going far beyond static image or video generation.
  • ByteDance demonstrated driving scenario simulation and household robot training, signaling applications far beyond consumer content creation.
  • With NVIDIA Cosmos and World Labs (acquired by AMD for $8.2B) moving in parallel, world model technology is industrializing rapidly.
Industry Insight

Brands could generate explorable 3D campaign environments from a text brief alone, dramatically cutting visual production costs.

Read our full write-up

ByteDance unveiled its World Model at the 2026 Douyin Group tech showcase, demonstrating the ability to generate interactive, explorable three-dimensional spaces from a single text prompt. The system produces a miniature-perspective 3D room scene from a text description and allows users to freely navigate through it using keyboard and mouse, going well beyond static rendered images or video playback.

The demonstration highlighted three core technical capabilities. First, Spatial Representation: the system maintains geometric consistency throughout user exploration, preventing object misalignment or spatial incoherence as the viewpoint shifts. Second, Environment State Modeling and Dynamic Prediction: the system tracks real-time physical relationships between objects within the scene. In the live demo, users could summon objects — such as a spoon — and physically interact with them, with the system computing motion trajectories in real time. Third, Cross-Scene Continuity: a feature named the "Magic Door" (任意門) creates seamless transition portals between semantically unrelated scenes — for instance, connecting an indoor room to outer space — enabling continuous cross-boundary exploration.

Technically, the World Model builds on foundations established by ByteDance's prior generative AI work. The video generation model Seedance 2.5 contributed spatial consistency and controllable editing capabilities, while the image generation model Seedream 5.0 Pro provided detailed scene rendering. Together these underpin the spatial and temporal coherence demands of a world model, which requires simulating physical rule evolution across a 3D environment rather than merely generating individual frames.

As of the announcement, the World Model has not been publicly released, and ByteDance has not disclosed a commercialization timeline. Industry observers note that if interactive world models reach production maturity, they could unlock structural applications in game level design, VR content production, architectural visualization, and embodied AI training data generation. For the marketing industry specifically, the ability to rapidly generate virtual environments at low cost could reshape advertising production workflows, reducing reliance on physical set construction and location shoots.

MiniCPM-V 4.7 Open-Sourced: 35B Multimodal Model Runs Offline on iPhone with Just 3GB RAM

Key Highlights
  • MiniCPM-V 4.7 scored 94.24 on OmniDocBench v1.6, outperforming competitors by more than 12 points on document parsing.
  • The 35B-A3B version uses a mixture-of-experts architecture activating only ~3B parameters per inference, fitting within 3GB RAM on iPhone 17 Pro Max.
  • 'Self-Replay Fine-Tuning' lets teams adapt the model to brand-specific tasks while preserving general capabilities, lowering the bar for custom on-device AI.
Industry Insight

Ad agencies can analyze client briefs, contracts, and creative assets locally with zero cloud exposure, enabling privacy-first AI workflows on existing devices.

Read our full write-up

ModelBest and OpenBMB jointly released MiniCPM-V 4.7, an open-source multimodal model available in two variants. The flagship 35B-A3B version uses a Mixture of Experts (MoE) architecture with a nominal 35 billion parameters, but activates only approximately 3 billion parameters per inference token, compressing memory usage to around 3GB. A 1.3B fully-activated variant is also available for extremely resource-constrained deployments. The 35B-A3B version has been validated running fully offline on an iPhone 17 Pro Max at over 13 tokens per second, requiring no internet or cloud server connection.

The model achieves competitive results on major benchmarks. It scores 91.72 on MMBench, 80.22 on MMMU (which evaluates multi-discipline understanding), and 94.24 on OmniDocBench v1.6 — outperforming competitors by more than 12 points on the document understanding benchmark. Testing on an iPad Pro M4 demonstrated a 5x decoding speed improvement versus the prior generation.

Three technical innovations underpin this performance profile. Look-ahead Expert Scheduling (LES) predicts which expert modules the next transformer layer will require while the current layer is still computing, achieving over 80% prefetch accuracy and substantially reducing the inference latency inherent to MoE architectures. Mixed-precision quantization applies differentiated weight compression strategies layer by layer, preserving representation fidelity in precision-sensitive layers while aggressively compressing others to minimize model size. Self-Replay Fine-Tuning addresses the well-documented capability forgetting problem that occurs when models are fine-tuned for specific tasks, implementing a knowledge-retention mechanism that preserves general capabilities during customization.

The model supports iOS, Android, and HarmonyOS, and is compatible with SGLang, vLLM, llama.cpp, and Ollama inference frameworks. For the advertising and marketing industry, on-device multimodal inference without cloud data transmission opens deployment paths for image content understanding, audience behavior analysis, and personalized content generation in compliance-sensitive contexts — including financial services, healthcare, and enterprise intranets — where uploading user data to external servers is restricted or prohibited.

PepsiCo Moves $1.9B Media Account to Publicis Without a Pitch, Ending 30-Year Omnicom Relationship

Key Highlights
  • PepsiCo terminated a 30-year Omnicom relationship without a formal pitch, directly appointing Publicis Groupe.
  • Publicis pitched directly to the CEO level, bypassing CMOs — reshaping how agencies develop new business.
  • Coca-Cola is reportedly reviewing its North American account, with Omnicom and Dentsu in talks, signaling a wider industry ripple effect.
Industry Insight

Data capability is replacing creative execution as the primary criterion for client agency selection — a structural shift for the industry.

Read our full write-up

PepsiCo transferred its global media buying account to Publicis Groupe without holding any formal competitive pitch, ending a three-decade relationship with Omnicom Group. According to estimates from media research firm COMvergence, PepsiCo's annual global media spend totals approximately $1.9 billion, with around $780 million attributable to North America alone — making this one of the largest direct account transfers in the advertising industry in recent years.

PepsiCo CEO Ramon Laguarta personally endorsed the decision on an earnings call, describing Publicis as "an important capability for us" and promising the partnership would deliver "much more data, much more granularity." Industry analysts noted that account switches of this magnitude are typically championed by the Chief Marketing Officer, not the CEO; Laguarta's direct and public endorsement on an investor call is an unusually strong signal that PepsiCo is repositioning advertising investment from a standard operating cost to a core strategic lever under his direct ownership.

The switch comes as PepsiCo's North America business faces volume declines following years of consumer price increases. Management requires more precisely targeted media investment to support brand recovery. Publicis has invested heavily in first-party data integration and programmatic buying capabilities, with its Epsilon data platform widely regarded as a key differentiating asset — a significant factor in attracting the account away from Omnicom.

The ripple effects are already visible. Coca-Cola, which had recently moved to Publicis from WPP, is now reviewing its North American media account and reportedly in discussions with Omnicom and Dentsu. The broader industry expectation is that as holdco consolidation continues, large brands will increasingly evaluate agency partners primarily on data capability and technology platform integration — further compressing the competitive leverage of traditional creative services in major account competitions.

ADK and Stagwell Formalize Three-Year Global Partnership, Using AI to Take Japanese IP Worldwide

Key Highlights
  • The partnership builds on a July 2025 MOU, formalized after roughly one year of joint project testing.
  • The focus is expanding Japanese IP into global markets (starting North America) and helping international brands enter Japan, combining ADK's local expertise with Stagwell Media's AI buying platform.
  • The 'test first, then formalize' approach offers a replicable framework for APAC agencies building cross-border alliances.
Industry Insight

Japanese IP global expansion creates cross-border business opportunities for APAC agencies to pursue or compete against.

Read our full write-up

ADK Marketing Solutions, the core marketing arm of ADK Holdings, and global marketing network Stagwell formally announced a three-year Master Agreement on October 13, 2026, elevating their relationship from project-based cooperation to a long-term strategic framework. The two companies had signed a Memorandum of Understanding in July 2025, and after roughly one year of joint project collaboration, both sides determined the partnership was strong enough to warrant a more institutionalized structure.

The agreement is organized around two primary business directions. The first is globalizing Japanese intellectual property, with North America as the initial target market: ADK leads IP operations while Stagwell contributes its U.S. data analytics capabilities and agency network. The second is supporting international brands entering Japan, where both parties will jointly develop localized marketing strategies. Beyond project collaboration, the agreement includes structured personnel exchanges and shared access to data, tools, and marketing solutions across both networks.

ADK Marketing Solutions Executive Vice President of Global Marketing Masato Suematsu stated that the prior year of collaboration had built sufficient mutual trust to move toward a more formal strategic framework. Stagwell APAC Managing Director Randy Duax described the partnership's logic directly: 'ADK brings an unmatched understanding of Japan...while Stagwell brings a global network built to transform marketing through AI.' This framing positions Stagwell's AI capabilities as its central contribution to the deal.

The strategic context is significant. Japanese anime, gaming, and virtual talent IP has seen sustained global demand growth, yet Japanese advertising agencies have historically lacked the scaled distribution infrastructure and marketing tooling needed outside Japan. ADK's choice to partner with Stagwell's North American AI data and media buying network — rather than building its own international structure — reflects a broader reassessment of globalization pathways within Japan's advertising industry. Financial terms of the agreement were not disclosed.

OpenAI Bets on Field Marketing for Enterprise ChatGPT Sales: In-Person Events Beat Digital Ads

Key Highlights
  • OpenAI EMEA B2B marketing head Leila Woodington identifies in-person field events as the top enterprise conversion channel, with no single channel able to persuade Fortune 500 executives alone.
  • OpenAI's mix includes creator partnerships limited to genuine product users, ~2 webinars per month, and local-language regional teams for market penetration.
  • Instagram sponsored posts doubled from 61 in June to 122 in July, showing continued digital investment for brand awareness even as field events drive enterprise conversion.
Industry Insight

OpenAI's channel mix reveals complex B2B AI sales still require in-person trust-building — a lesson for any agency pitching AI-led services.

Read our full write-up

Leila Woodington, OpenAI's Head of B2B Marketing for EMEA, has identified in-person field marketing as 'the one I would definitely put out as our strongest channel right now' among all channels used to sell ChatGPT Enterprise. Woodington spent over a decade building Facebook's B2B marketing function before moving through roles at Snap and an AI video startup, then joined OpenAI more than a year ago to build its enterprise marketing capability across Europe, the Middle East, and Africa.

Woodington explained the structural reason why field marketing cannot be replaced by digital advertising in enterprise sales: there is a meaningful gap between consumer-level brand awareness and actual enterprise purchasing decisions. Even a Fortune 500 executive who is familiar with ChatGPT as a product does not automatically know how to deploy it across thousands of employees or how to persuade multiple layers of executive decision-makers. That complex buying process requires direct human interaction to advance effectively.

Operationally, the OpenAI marketing team uses a predefined activity calendar rather than building fresh campaigns for each product launch. When the persistent AI agent product Dots launched last month in San Francisco, its promotion was plugged into existing campaign frameworks rather than requiring a standalone build. Beyond field events, the team runs approximately two webinars per month on a fixed schedule and works with 'carefully curated' influencer and creator partners who genuinely use the products. Instagram sponsored posts doubled from 61 in June 2026 to 122 in July, according to Business Insider data.

Woodington acknowledged a notable tension: enterprise CMOs expect her to have mastered every OpenAI tool, yet her own team faces the same pace challenge as any other organization — 'we are trying to keep up in the same way as others.' This admission surfaces a specific difficulty in AI product marketing: the marketers themselves are simultaneously users and learners of a rapidly iterating product, not merely its communicators.

How Brands Get Cited by AI: Flo Health and Motorway's GEO Playbook Unpacked

Key Highlights
  • Motorway tracks LLM outputs in real time and sees new content cited by AI within 24 hours of publication; it is already testing ChatGPT ads in the UK, launched May 2026.
  • Flo Health hired a dedicated LLM discoverability manager who collaborates across every channel during media planning and messaging development.
  • Effective GEO requires three layers: real-time AI citation monitoring, assigned ownership of LLM discoverability, and testing paid placements on AI answer platforms.
Industry Insight

With roughly 1-in-10 global searches now on AI tools, GEO is becoming a billable skill agencies must build urgently.

Read our full write-up

At the Semrush Spotlight event in London on October 13, 2026, women's health app Flo Health and used car trading platform Motorway separately disclosed their practical strategies for generative engine optimization (GEO) — the discipline of getting brands cited by AI tools such as ChatGPT and Perplexity.

Motorway Marketing and Brand Director Cassie Hall described a real-time tracking mechanism: the company continuously monitors large language model outputs to identify in which query scenarios the brand is cited and where it is absent. Hall stated, 'We can publish new content and see it being cited within 24 hours' — a cycle far shorter than the weeks or months traditional SEO typically requires, reflecting the fact that language model crawling and index updating now operates close to real time. Motorway is also testing ChatGPT ads, which launched in the U.K. in May 2026, providing a paid pathway to reach users engaging with AI-generated answers.

Flo Health's strategy centers on organizational restructuring rather than purely technical content optimization. Head of Brand Marketing Georgia Jones revealed that the company has hired a dedicated LLM Discoverability manager whose remit spans every channel, with involvement at the point where media plans and messaging frameworks are developed. The underlying logic is that the primary driver of AI citations is brand message consistency across touchpoints, not technical optimization on any single page. Where a brand delivers contradictory information across platforms, language models generating citations will tend to favor competitors whose messaging is clearer and more authoritative.

Microsoft Advertising EMEA Regional VP of Sales Helena Dillon also participated in the event. Together, the two cases point toward a shared operational principle for GEO: brands need to build verifiable message consistency infrastructure and treat AI citation as a measurable, optimizable channel — rather than passively waiting for language models to learn about them on their own.

Google Demand Gen October Drop: Data Strength Uplift Metric Launches, Data Manager Expands to DV360

Key Highlights
  • The new Data Strength Uplift metric lets advertisers quantify conversion gains from first-party data setup without leaving the Google Ads interface.
  • Data Manager is expanding to Display & Video 360 and Google Analytics, enabling unified first-party data management across Google's ecosystem.
  • Insurance platform The Zebra achieved 16% more conversions and 19% higher conversion value after implementing data strength best practices.
Industry Insight

First time advertisers can quantify how much first-party data setup lifts conversion volume directly in-platform.

Read our full write-up

Google published its 14th Demand Gen Drop on October 13, 2026, releasing several updates centered on the concept of 'data strength' — with the core objective of enabling advertisers to quantify the actual contribution of their first-party data configuration to campaign performance, and extending that data infrastructure framework to a broader set of Google ecosystem platforms.

The most substantive new feature is the Data Strength Uplift Metric, set to launch in the Google Ads interface. This metric will show advertisers directly how much additional conversion uplift their current data configuration — including conversion tracking, Customer Match, and Enhanced Conversions setup — is generating within Demand Gen campaigns, giving them a more intuitive way to evaluate the return on their data investments. A refreshed Data Strength Best Practices Guide with actionable configuration steps was released alongside the feature.

At the infrastructure level, Google announced the expansion of Data Manager to Display & Video 360 (DV360) and Google Analytics, with Enhanced Conversions also coming to both platforms in the near term. Previously, Data Manager was primarily available within the native Google Ads environment; this expansion means advertisers can apply the same first-party data management framework within programmatic buying and analytics platforms. Google internal analysis — using a study window of June 3 to 17, 2026 — found that advertisers using Google Tag Gateway see an average of over 20% conversion uplift in Demand Gen campaigns.

U.S. insurance comparison platform The Zebra provided a concrete real-world reference point: after implementing data strength optimizations, the platform achieved a 16% increase in conversion volume and a 19% increase in overall conversion value. Both figures serve as the primary external proof points for Google's update announcement. Taken together, the direction of this update is to reframe first-party data configuration from a technical compliance matter into a quantifiable performance lever within advertising campaigns.
