Walmart Opens Agent Gateway for AI Shoppers While Amazon Actively Blocks Them

Key Highlights
  • Walmart launched an MCP server called Agent Gateway, allowing approved AI agents including ChatGPT and Gemini to complete purchases on behalf of users, with daily spending limits and privacy controls.
  • Amazon is taking the opposite approach, actively blocking AI agents from completing purchases and requiring human checkout.
  • The strategic divergence signals a new competitive frontier for brands: 'agent discoverability' — surfacing in AI agent shopping recommendations — may become the next critical optimization metric.
Industry Insight

Agentic commerce is arriving; brand e-commerce ad strategy and audience targeting logic will need a fundamental rethink.

Pattern Watch

Whether a brand's products surface in AI agent shopping recommendations will define reach in this new consumer channel — the SEO competition logic of the past may replay in a new form for the agentic commerce era.

Read our full write-up

Walmart's Agent Gateway uses the Model Context Protocol (MCP), allowing authorized AI agents — currently including ChatGPT and Google Gemini — to complete purchases through a consumer's Wallet account without the user opening the app or re-entering payment details. Daily spending limits and privacy settings are configured by the consumer, meaning the entire authorization flow is an explicit opt-in rather than a default.

Amazon is taking the exact opposite position, quietly tightening system restrictions on AI agents completing automated purchases and requiring human confirmation at the final step. The divergence has a clear commercial logic: Amazon's ad revenue depends heavily on its Sponsored Products auction, and AI agent intermediation threatens to bypass real-time consumer behavior signals that make ad targeting precise. Walmart's own ad business is far smaller, and its e-commerce operation is in structural catch-up mode — the incremental traffic from agentic commerce is worth more than protecting an existing ad ecosystem.

For brands, this is a front that requires early preparation. When AI agents select products to recommend, they evaluate structured data — description clarity, ratings, inventory, and pricing — not real-time ad bids. The emerging logic is closer to SEO than programmatic advertising. Brands that optimize their product data for AI agent discoverability on Walmart's platform today will carry that advantage when Amazon eventually opens the same door.

Nielsen Survey: Over Half of Executives Can't Evaluate AI-Assisted Work, Tool Sprawl Worsens

Key Highlights
  • A Nielsen survey of 1,700 senior leaders found over 50% cannot reliably evaluate the actual quality and true contribution of AI-assisted employee work.
  • AI tool sprawl increased over the past year rather than consolidating, and managers are now expected to assess employees' prompt engineering skills in performance reviews.
  • 39% of respondents used AI to draft performance reviews, but the accuracy of AI-assisted evaluation mechanisms remains unverified and carries systemic bias risk.
Industry Insight

Agencies must establish clear AI workflow quality review mechanisms, or they cannot credibly assure clients of output quality.

Read our full write-up

Nielsen's survey of 1,700 senior leaders found over 50% openly acknowledge difficulty evaluating the true quality of AI-assisted employee work. The root cause is structural: when employees use AI to assist with reports, analysis, or pitches, the output may appear more polished and better formatted, yet not necessarily more accurate or insightful than unassisted work. Managers face 'improved-looking outputs' with no reliable method to distinguish AI polish from genuine thinking.

The survey also reveals a paradox: AI tool sprawl is increasing rather than consolidating, with the average employee now using more than three AI tools. Managers are expected to evaluate employees' 'prompt engineering skills' in performance reviews — yet 39% of surveyed executives admitted they already use AI to draft those performance reviews. This creates a double-dependency: using AI to evaluate work done with AI, where any bias in the evaluation chain gets amplified rather than corrected.

For agencies, the implication is that internal quality control frameworks need to be built proactively, not assembled in response to client questions. Practically, this means defining which types of work are AI-assisted, which require fully human execution, and building auditable review records. That mechanism protects delivery quality and is also the most persuasive way to position an agency as a 'trustworthy AI work partner' to clients who are wrestling with exactly the same management challenge.

Meta Q3 Ad Revenue Hits $63.1B: AI Tools Boost Conversions 9.6%, Agent Shopping Averages $200+

Since Yesterday

On October 21 we reported Meta Muse's 40-day milestone of 10M US downloads with 30% 7-day retention — the user growth side. Today's Q3 earnings call revealed the commerce side: the AI GEM model lifted app ad conversions 9.6%, agent shopping averaged $200+ per order, and partnership ads surged 97%+ YoY. Zuckerberg also signaled future transaction fees on Muse commerce — the free window for brands on this channel may close sooner than expected.

Key Highlights
  • Meta Q3 ad revenue reached $63.1B, up 26% year-over-year, beating market expectations; total Q3 revenue was $64.6B.
  • The AI GEM model boosted app ad conversion rates by 9.6%, with nearly 10 million advertisers now using generative AI creative tools.
  • Muse agent shopping averaged over $200 per order; partnership ads surged 97%+ YoY to a $15B annual run rate, with Zuckerberg signaling future transaction fee collection.
Industry Insight

Meta AI ad tool performance is now earnings-validated; it's time to assess Advantage+ and GEM model adoption for clients.

Pattern Watch

Zuckerberg's transaction fee signal means Muse's free commerce window may be closing. Brands that optimize product data and agent shopping flows now stand to hold an early-mover advantage when the commission model launches.

Read our full write-up

Meta Q3 results matter directly to advertising agencies because they translate AI ad tool performance into auditable, earnings-call numbers for the first time. The AI GEM model's 9.6% lift in app ad conversion rates was not a lab benchmark — it was a real-world result across nearly 10 million advertisers. Advertisers running Advantage+ Shopping campaigns saw meaningful efficiency improvements in Q3, giving agencies a concrete, defensible basis to recommend migration to AI-optimized automated campaigns to clients who have been hesitant.

Muse commerce data carries a separate signal. Average order values above $200 for agent shopping purchases indicate that Muse's user base has moved from novelty early adopters to consumers with real purchase intent. Partnership Ads' 97%+ YoY growth to a $15B annual run rate reflects accelerating brand investment in social commerce. Zuckerberg's explicit foreshadowing of future transaction fees on Muse agent purchases is notable: it means the timeline for this channel transitioning from free to paid is no longer speculative.

For agencies, three actions are worth prioritizing immediately: first, audit how many client campaigns have not yet migrated to Advantage+, and begin transitioning them with Q3 data as justification; second, evaluate Partnership Ads potential for clients with D2C e-commerce, where the entry cost is lower now than it will be in 12 months; third, verify client product data completeness on Muse — building that foundation before the commission model launches is the most cost-effective preparation strategy.

Prebid Proposes Inverting the Ad Auction: Google GAM Bids Into Prebid, Targeting Early 2027

Key Highlights
  • Prebid proposed a new auction architecture where Google Ad Manager bids into Prebid's auction, replacing the current setup where Prebid bids into GAM for unfilled inventory.
  • The architecture aims to achieve functional separation of GAM and AdX while bypassing court-ordered divestiture; Google has already attempted to roll back court-mandated provisions.
  • Prebid targets early 2027 for formalization; Apple iOS's concurrent blocking of identity resolution firms is intensifying adtech concentration, with both forces reshaping the bidding landscape.
Industry Insight

Programmatic ad bidding logic faces structural reshaping; agencies should proactively assess DSP strategy contingencies.

Read our full write-up

Prebid's 'auction flip' proposal targets a long-standing structural advantage: in the current setup, Prebid's auction only gets access to inventory Google Ad Manager couldn't fill, effectively giving Google 'last look' rights on its own platform. The proposed architecture inverts this relationship — GAM would participate in Prebid's auction as a standard bidder, competing with other DSPs on equal terms, with Prebid maintaining final arbitration as the neutral auction layer.

The proposal is framed against the ongoing Google adtech antitrust proceedings. Courts have ruled for forced separation of GAM and AdX, with structural restrictions on Google's adtech business. Google is actively pushing back to narrow the scope of court-mandated provisions. Prebid's proposal is essentially an industry self-regulation alternative: if GAM can participate as a fair bidder in Prebid's auction, the judicial forced divestiture process may become less urgent. The target timeline for formalization is early 2027.

The direct impact for agencies has two dimensions. In the near term, this architecture change remains in proposal form and requires no immediate adjustments to programmatic buying strategy. In the medium term, if Prebid's proposal is adopted, Google's pricing leverage in supply-side adtech will be systematically reduced, improving DSP parity in programmatic bidding and lifting overall cost efficiency. Agencies should track litigation progress and Google's response, and preserve strategic flexibility in their 2027 budget planning.

Netflix Names The Trade Desk as Programmatic Partner, Launches Pixel; Lowers 2026 Ad Target 10%

Key Highlights
  • Netflix lowered its 2026 ad revenue target by 10%, from $3B to $2.7B, citing slower-than-planned rollout of advertising capabilities.
  • The Trade Desk is named a new programmatic buying partner; Netflix also launched Netflix Pixel for conversion tracking and a Conversion API for first-party data matching.
  • Despite the lowered target, Netflix's ad business still grew over 80% year-over-year, with Q3 total revenue reaching $12.9B and ad-supported tier subscribers continuing to grow.
Industry Insight

Netflix's programmatic expansion means CTV media plans can now include The Trade Desk buying paths.

Read our full write-up

Netflix's ad revenue target reduction stems from slower-than-planned rollout of advertising capabilities — including frequency management, audience targeting tools, and programmatic buying interfaces — rather than weak growth in ad-supported tier subscribers, which continued to grow healthily. The bottleneck is on the supply side (ad tool maturity), not the demand side.

Adding The Trade Desk as a new programmatic partner represents an important expansion of Netflix's advertising technology infrastructure. Previously, Netflix's programmatic access was relatively concentrated; the new partnership allows buyers to purchase Netflix inventory through more standardized DSP workflows, reducing the entry barrier for programmatic activation. The launch of Netflix Pixel and Conversion API addresses the attribution gap, enabling advertisers to measure how Netflix ads contribute to off-platform conversion outcomes.

For agencies managing CTV budgets, late 2026 and early 2027 represent the right window to reassess Netflix. Ad tool maturity is progressively improving, The Trade Desk channel reduces purchasing complexity, and the growing ad-supported subscriber base ensures meaningful inventory scale. Netflix's ad business still grew over 80% year-over-year; the target reduction reflects management conservatism more than an underlying business problem.

YouTube Commands 44% of Streaming Ad Exposure Time; Tubi Ranks Second at 12%

Key Highlights
  • Comscore's August 2026 data shows YouTube commanded 44% of all streaming platform ad exposure time, maintaining an absolute lead.
  • Tubi ranked second at 12%; Netflix, Amazon Prime Video, and Peacock compete for third, with rankings heavily influenced by football and sports seasons.
  • Overall streaming ad exposure still represents just 21% of total TV advertising, below its share of total viewing time, indicating significant room for CTV ad inventory growth.
Industry Insight

YouTube is the most efficient streaming ad channel; concrete data now supports increasing CTV budget weighting.

Read our full write-up

Comscore's ad exposure time share data carries a systematic gap relative to overall viewership share data: streaming platforms' ad exposure share is substantially below their viewership share, because a significant portion of subscribers use ad-free tiers and many platforms' ad-supported tiers are relatively new. YouTube commands 44% of ad exposure time partly because virtually all its content carries advertising, and partly because it already leads total viewing time on streaming platforms.

Tubi ranking second at 12% — well above its share of total viewership — reflects the structural characteristics of FAST (Free Ad-Supported Streaming) channels: Tubi's entire inventory is ad-supported, and its ad load (ads per viewing hour) typically exceeds SVOD-to-ad-tier competitors. Netflix, Amazon Prime Video, and Peacock face heavy seasonal volatility in their ad exposure rankings, with NFL and NBA content capable of sharply shifting the numbers within a single quarter.

For media planning, this data has three practical implications: first, prioritizing YouTube's CTV inventory in streaming ad mixes is the highest-efficiency default starting point; second, FAST channels (led by Tubi) suit brand awareness budgets seeking broad reach rather than precision targeting; third, streaming ad exposure still represents only 21% of total TV advertising, confirming that CTV inventory has substantial room to grow — the decision to increase CTV budget allocations in recent years looks correct in retrospect.

Recursive Launches Flint: Open-Source Laptop Coding Model Largely Built by AI

Key Highlights
  • Flint was largely built by Recursive's Eureka Machine AI research system, which autonomously identifies coding error patterns and generates corresponding training data.
  • It achieves 131 tokens/second on an M4 Pro MacBook and outperforms all comparable open-source models under 50B parameters on SWE-Bench Verified.
  • Flint is fully open-source under Apache 2.0, runs locally on-device, and requires no code upload to the cloud, making it suitable for privacy-sensitive workflows.
Industry Insight

Mature local lightweight models enable agencies to explore on-device AI to reduce costs and protect client data privacy.

Read our full write-up

Flint's construction methodology is arguably more significant than its utility as a standalone tool. Recursive's Eureka Machine AI research system autonomously identifies common coding error patterns, generates corresponding training data, and iterates the model. This AI-training-AI architecture means model improvement velocity can theoretically outpace human-labeled data preparation, while the marginal cost of the training pipeline continues to fall.

Performance-wise, Flint achieves 131 tokens/second on an M4 Pro MacBook and outperforms all comparable open-source models under 50 billion parameters on SWE-Bench Verified. More importantly, it runs entirely on a consumer laptop without GPU servers or cloud API calls — directly addressing the core concern of creative and technical workers who are unwilling to upload sensitive code to third-party services. Flint is Apache 2.0 licensed with no commercial usage fees.

For advertising and marketing agencies, Flint's relevance lies not in displacing existing AI writing tools, but in opening a new category of use cases: locally-run code assistance that can handle technical tasks involving CRM data, ad trafficking scripts, or client reporting automation without transmitting client data offsite. As on-device model technology continues to mature, agencies can begin exploring device-side AI for sensitive workflows — and Flint is currently one of the lowest-cost entry points in this space.

Forbes Joins Google AI News Pilot: Trading Content Rights for AI Citations Amid 45% Traffic Drop

Key Highlights
  • Forbes joined Google's paid News AI pilot, granting Google expanded content rights in exchange for AI Overview summaries, audio briefings, and attribution links for its articles.
  • Forbes saw roughly a 45% year-over-year traffic decline and is actively pivoting to subscriptions, events, and diversified revenue; joining the AI pilot also provides user insight data.
  • Google's AI news program now covers 400+ publishers, signaling that the shift from traditional search clicks to AI citation has created systemic impacts across the media landscape.
Industry Insight

AI citation traffic is replacing traditional click-through for media; content marketing and SEO strategies need to adapt accordingly.

Read our full write-up

Forbes joining Google's paid News AI pilot is essentially a public pricing test for publishers in the AI search era. The core logic of traditional SEO was to appear on the first page of search results, drive clicks, and generate ad impressions or subscription conversions. Google AI Overview is systematically eroding this logic — once users receive AI-generated summaries, their motivation to click through to original sources drops significantly. Forbes's roughly 45% traffic decline directly reflects this trend.

By joining the paid program, Forbes chose the strategy of 'if you're going to be summarized, get paid for licensing the summaries' — in exchange for AI citation links, audio briefing inclusion, and user behavior insights. The specific financial terms were not disclosed, but Google's equivalent programs now span 400+ publishers, suggesting the industry has reached a working market price consensus. This represents a pragmatic path for publishers dealing with AI traffic erosion, alongside the copyright litigation route.

The implication for brand content marketing and SEO strategy is that if mainstream media is already restructuring content for 'AI citation visibility' rather than 'click traffic,' brand-owned media content needs to follow the same logic. Future KPI frameworks may need to incorporate an 'AI citation rate' — how frequently and accurately brand-relevant topics appear in Google AI Overview and similar systems — rather than tracking organic search clicks alone. Agencies that build client AI citation monitoring frameworks early will be positioned ahead of the next cycle of SEO tool evolution.
