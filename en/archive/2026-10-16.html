GPT-6-Astra solves 49/50 physics research problems at just $2.40 each

Key Highlights
  • 98% accuracy (49/50) significantly surpasses competitors: Opus 5.5 at 92%, Gemini 4 Pro at 82%.
  • Cost of just $2.40 per problem vs. prior $18 benchmark dramatically lowers the barrier to scale.
  • OpenAI simultaneously launched a research API tier, pushing AI deeper into data analytics and ad attribution tools.
Industry Insight

The capability gap between frontier models is widening rapidly, compressing the tool selection window for agencies that rely on complex AI-powered analytics.

Read our full write-up

TheoPhysEval was designed by 35 researchers from ICTS, Harvard, KU Leuven, and Google DeepMind. Its 50 problems span 11 subfields of theoretical physics — including fluid dynamics, cosmology, and particle physics — and are deliberately framed as 'turn-the-crank' tasks: applying known methods to modified questions rather than open-ended derivation, so that definitive correct answers exist. All problems are publicly available on Hugging Face. Notably, GPT-6-Astra's one apparent error was later traced to a mistake in the answer key itself; once corrected, Astra's effective accuracy is arguably 100%.

The cost shift is commercially significant. Prior AI-assisted solutions to comparable physics problems cost around $18 per problem; GPT-6-Astra completed all 50 for approximately $116 total, averaging $2.40 each — an 87% cost reduction. Each model ran twice, once with and once without web search. Without search access, Astra still scored 92%, indicating that leading models have substantially internalised the theoretical physics literature and do not depend on real-time retrieval.

The competitive gap is wide. Anthropic Opus 5.5 in Claude Code mode scored 92% for second place; Google Gemini 3.8 Flash reached 68%; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 54%; GLM 5.3 42%. Weaker models made concrete errors — applying five-dimensional formulae when four-dimensional solutions were sufficient — revealing structural differences in physics reasoning, not merely marginal score gaps.

The benchmark authors offer a pointed observation: AI agents are now computationally powerful but lack scientific 'taste' — the judgment to identify which questions are worth pursuing. This explains why strong benchmark scores have not yet translated into major physics breakthroughs. For marketing science practitioners, the implication is parallel: AI can now dramatically reduce the cost of structured analytical work, but problem framing and hypothesis generation still require human domain expertise.

Industry Insight

Model roadmap uncertainty increases; agencies built on GPT-6-Astra can safely maintain their tool stack without urgent migration pressure.

Read our full write-up

The OpenAI flagship delay stems from two compounding structural problems. The first is an alignment gap: the model's behaviour does not yet reliably match what researchers and enterprise customers expect. The second is an infrastructure gap: the evaluation and testing systems needed to safely certify the model are not yet robust enough to support a launch.

The deeper technical conflict is architectural. Both the flagship and GPT-6-Astra use Looped Transformers — branded internally as Recurrent Depth — where tokens pass through transformer layers multiple times before output. This improves performance on math and coding while reducing inference costs, but compresses reasoning into internal numerical states that human reviewers cannot read. Traditional chain-of-thought safety monitoring breaks down as a result. OpenAI deliberately capped Astra's loop count to preserve readable reasoning. Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki has publicly acknowledged that the current monitoring approach is growing 'fragile' and 'unfortunately trending in a negative direction.'

Policy constraints add a further timing layer. Under a US executive order, OpenAI must provide the US government up to 30 days of pre-release access before outside partners, and a longer window before any public launch — meaning technical readiness alone does not determine release dates.

Following a security incident, OpenAI redirected roughly 10% of total compute to alignment and safety work. At market electricity rates, this represents several billion dollars annually, signalling that alignment has been elevated to a priority on par with product development.

For enterprise buyers and adtech platform vendors, the practical implication is that GPT-6-Turbo remains the stable commercial API for the foreseeable future. Roadmap planning that treats the flagship's availability as a fixed milestone rather than an uncertain variable carries real schedule risk, particularly as Anthropic and Google continue shipping their own frontier releases.

Anthropic's independent evaluation framework stems from commitments made in the White House Accord on Super Intelligence and represents the industry's first formal structure granting third-party safety evaluators employee-level access. METR, a nonprofit specialising in frontier AI evaluation, holds the highest access tier: six METR staff received Anthropic office space and hardware, with access comparable to Anthropic's own internal risk teams. The three-tier framework creates a clear trust gradient. Tier 1 evaluators operate under Anthropic publication approval with narrow scope. Tier 2 (SecureBio, Accenture, Faculty) publish independently with limited Anthropic editorial rights. Tier 3 — METR's current status — combines employee-like access with full publication independence. SecureBio handles the biosecurity chapter of Anthropic's August 2026 Risk Report; Accenture and Faculty conduct red-teaming, alignment assessments, and safeguard testing; Hugging Face remains in early-stage discussions.

Evaluator access is deliberately bounded: teams are blocked from customer data, attorney-client privileged information, and model architecture details where inadvertent leak risk exists. This scoping ensures the evaluation framework does not itself become a security exposure for Anthropic's partners.

Anthropicexplicitly states that every frontier AI company should adopt equivalent practices, using the bank examiner analogy — resident examiners inside major financial institutions — to position independent AI evaluation as an emerging industry baseline.

For enterprise procurement teams, the practical value is citable third-party validation. Published independent risk assessments allow procurement and legal teams to reference non-vendor safety conclusions in due diligence documentation. Reports that Google and Meta are pursuing similar frameworks suggest that independent evaluation may soon become a de facto requirement for enterprise AI procurement, raising the compliance bar for vendors who lack such credentials.

Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 Argon advances a single-model architecture for the full robotics pipeline: simulation generation, robot control, and training data evaluation all handled by one model. The design aims to eliminate the integration overhead and latency costs of multi-model robotic systems.

The most technically compelling demonstration is simulation generation from minimal inputs. A single photograph produced over 4,500 training episodes, lifting a real robot's task success rate from 56% to 75%. In a self-improvement loop, a robot arm reduced task execution time from roughly 59 seconds to approximately 17 seconds across three self-corrected iterations — a 71% reduction. Google's Universal Cart marks a strategic inflection point: the company is no longer content to own the discovery phase of shopping but is now embedding itself directly into the transaction layer. Available across Google Search and the Gemini app, the tool lets users save products from multiple retailers into a single persistent cart, eliminating the need to hunt down items again when ready to buy. This architecture is significant: it means Gemini and future AI agents could in principle execute cross-merchant purchases on a user's behalf rather than simply making recommendations. Previously, conversion tracking often broke once a user left Google's ecosystem to complete a purchase on a retailer's own site. With Universal Cart, the full path from ad impression through add-to-cart to completed checkout can be recorded within a single infrastructure, materially improving ROAS calculation accuracy. The cart also surfaces relevant credit card perks and promo codes at checkout, embedding a layer of intelligent purchase incentive at the final step.

Campaign Asia: Apple Safari adtech blocking escalates, industry says it has gone too far

Since Yesterday

On October 13 we reported Safari's ad-tracking block driving eCPMs down over 10% in week one, with the industry scrambling to quantify losses. Today Campaign Asia goes deeper: Privacy Manifests now require ad SDKs to declare all tracking APIs, with multiple DSPs and SSPs calling for full compliance overhauls and criticising Apple's approach as disproportionate.

Key Highlights
  • Privacy Manifests require ad SDKs to declare all tracking APIs; non-compliant SDKs will be rejected by the App Store.
  • Affected technologies include retargeting pixels, third-party ID graphs, and some in-app ad measurement solutions.
  • Industry broadly recommends accelerating server-side tracking and first-party data infrastructure.
Industry Insight

iOS traffic attribution accuracy drops further; audience analysis and retargeting tools relying on Safari need re-evaluation, with server-side tracking becoming the recommended path.

Read our full write-up

Apple's latest adtech restrictions represent more than an incremental privacy update — they constitute a deliberate reshaping of the mobile and browser advertising ecosystem. The Campaign Asia Sounding Board piece frames the central question starkly: has Apple overreached? The core mechanism at issue is Privacy Manifests, which require app developers and SDK providers to explicitly declare the purpose of every tracking API call. This effectively forces the entire SDK stack into the open, threatening the behavioral fingerprinting approaches that have proliferated since ATT reduced cross-app identifier availability.

This update extends the logic of the 2023 App Tracking Transparency framework but operates at a deeper infrastructure level. Where ATT governed explicit consent for cross-app tracking, Privacy Manifests target the SDK layer and restrict specific system APIs — including timezone, user defaults, and disk space queries — that have been repurposed as side-channel device identifiers. Industry criticism clusters around two concrete harms: Safari eCPMs dropped more than 10% in the first week following the update, signaling declining advertiser confidence in iOS inventory; and the recommended mitigation — server-side tracking — requires significant backend engineering investment that many mid-market clients cannot readily absorb.

The structural damage to third-party ID graphs is the longer-term concern. Audience data vendors that stitched together user journeys via cross-site cookies or IDFA now face shrinking coverage in Safari environments, directly eroding retargeting reach and precision. The irony noted by several analysts is that Apple's privacy stance functionally consolidates power with Meta and Google, whose walled-garden first-party data becomes relatively more valuable as independent measurement infrastructure degrades.

For Hong Kong agencies, where iOS market share is disproportionately high, the impact is acute. Priority actions include auditing Safari traffic share across client portfolios, assessing current tracking architectures for compliance gaps, and exploring Conversion API or server-side GTM deployments to preserve measurement fidelity as client-side tracking continues to erode.

Citizen Lab: governments are exploiting online ad infrastructure to install zero-click spyware

Key Highlights
  • Attacks exploit the WinURL field in RTB bid requests, delivering malicious code to target devices without displaying any ad.
  • Confirmed cases involve journalists and civil society organisations; government clients have not yet been fully named.
  • IAB Tech Lab says it is evaluating protocol changes; compliance pressure on the ad ecosystem is expected to increase over coming quarters.
Industry Insight

An escalating trust crisis in the ad ecosystem puts SSPs and ad exchanges under regulatory pressure; brands must assess brand-safety and compliance risks.

Read our full write-up

Citizen Lab's October 15, 2026 report documents the specific mechanism by which programmatic advertising infrastructure has been weaponized for targeted device compromise. The central actor is Discover-Tech, an Israeli firm whose ad platform Controlium operates as an intermediary layer between legitimate ad networks and mercenary spyware vendors — specifically Candiru (made by Integrity Labs) and Bindecy, both specializing in zero-click exploitation tools that compromise devices without any user interaction.

The technical attack vector exploits the WinURL field in the Open RTB protocol. In normal programmatic auction flow, WinURL is a legitimate callback address for win notifications. Controlium's method involves delivering malicious code through this field when a targeted device loads an ad request, achieving silent device compromise. Citizen Lab researcher Bill Marczak described this as the first confirmed instance of government-backed zero-click spyware delivered through online advertising infrastructure. Confirmed victims include at least 45 Windows machines at Al Jazeera, compromised between 2024 and 2025 via Candiru spyware delivered through malicious ads.

Discover-Tech's operational security failures accelerated the exposure: source code was inadvertently left on login pages, user login records were publicly downloadable without authentication, and billions of ad bidding records from March to June 2026 were accessible without restriction. Those records show ad delivery across tens of thousands of apps and websites, with reportedly targeted networks including militaries, governments, critical infrastructure agencies, the IAEA, and the ICC. Ad quality firm Confiant independently corroborated the findings with supplementary evidence of malicious ad delivery.

The implications for the advertising industry extend well beyond individual privacy. Open bidding protocols were designed for efficiency and scale, not security auditing. IAB Tech Lab is now evaluating protocol-level changes, though any standards revision faces significant backward-compatibility challenges across existing infrastructure. For advertisers and agencies, the actionable near-term response is to require media buying partners to articulate their Supply Path Optimization strategy and to audit whether brand advertising placements may inadvertently appear alongside inventory sources that have been exploited as attack vectors.
