Anthropic Proposes AI Safety Bill Allowing Court Orders to Block Dangerous Models

Key Highlights
  • Anthropic's draft bill proposes that courts could block or restrict deployment of AI models posing foreseeable lethal risk to 50 or more people.
  • Carveouts are included to protect academic research and lower-risk model deployments from the same standard.
  • The move positions Anthropic as a proponent of safety legislation, contrasting with the broader tech industry's resistance to AI regulation.
Industry Insight

AI regulation moves from framework to draft legislation; agency AI tools face growing compliance scrutiny.

Read our full write-up

On October 20, 2026, Anthropic presented a draft AI safety bill to the U.S. Congress, centered on a specific numerical threshold: if an AI model is assessed as posing a foreseeable risk of killing 50 or more people, or causing $1 billion or more in property damage, a federal court may issue an order to block the model's release or continued operation. The triggering risk scenarios are confined to three categories: assisting in the creation or release of biological weapons, a model escaping developer controls, and enabling large-scale cyberattacks.

The draft establishes an independent evaluation framework. Evaluators must register with the U.S. Commerce Department, with costs borne by AI labs — though the draft explicitly states that payment cannot be contingent on evaluation outcomes, preserving evaluator independence. If an evaluator identifies an imminent risk, they have a 72-hour window to notify the Commerce Department, which then petitions a D.C. federal judge for an injunction. Notably, the draft restricts individual federal agencies from unilaterally blocking any model; the judicial pathway is the required route.

The proposal closely mirrors California's Senate Bill 53, which used identical thresholds for 'catastrophic risk,' signaling Anthropic's intent to align federal legislation with existing state-level standards. An Anthropic spokesperson confirmed the bill reflects the company's Advanced AI Framework, which has been shared with lawmakers since June 2026.

Industry positions diverge sharply. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has argued no new AI laws are needed at this stage, while DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis proposed an independent standards body modeled on FINRA, the financial industry's self-regulatory organization. Anthropic's approach sits between the two: preserving market freedom while placing a judicial check as a last resort for extreme, high-casualty scenarios rather than regulating routine deployments. Whether the bill can attract sufficient Congressional support remains an open question.

ChatGPT Launches Locked Chats With PIN or Biometric Protection

Key Highlights
  • Users can lock specific ChatGPT conversations behind a PIN or device biometrics — fingerprint or Face ID — with settings stored locally on the device.
  • The feature is available on iOS, Android, and desktop ChatGPT, addressing concerns about private conversations visible on shared office devices.
  • Locking is opt-in and does not affect existing conversations; users decide which conversations require protection.
Industry Insight

Agencies using ChatGPT for client briefs and strategy docs gain an extra layer of on-device protection against accidental data exposure.

Read our full write-up

OpenAI began rolling out its Locked Chats feature on October 20, 2026, giving users the ability to restrict access to selected conversations. Three authentication methods are supported: a 4-digit PIN, Face ID, and Touch ID. Once locked, a conversation is hidden from the main chat history, pinned conversations, and search results, and relocated to a dedicated Locked Chats section — making it inaccessible to anyone else using the same device without completing authentication.

Unlike Temporary Chats, which disappear after the session ends, Locked Chats are persistent and can be reopened by the owner at any time upon successful verification. When the Memory feature is active, Locked Chats maintains its own separate memory pool: new messages exchanged within a locked conversation will not update the standard memory, preventing sensitive content from bleeding into the broader context that ChatGPT draws on in regular sessions. One important caveat: resetting the PIN permanently and irreversibly deletes all locked chats, associated files, and protected content.

Several conversation types are explicitly excluded from locking: temporary chats, archived chats, group chats, shared-link chats, scheduled task chats, meeting recording chats, and the ChatGPT for Healthcare variant. The feature does not add end-to-end encryption, does not alter OpenAI's underlying data retention policies, and does not change the 'Improve the model for everyone' data-sharing setting — meaning the privacy benefit is limited to access on the device level, not at the infrastructure level.

The rollout covers all seven subscription tiers — Free, Go, Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu. OpenAI frames the primary use case as shared-device scenarios, such as planning a surprise for a family member, managing personal conversations, or protecting commercially sensitive workplace discussions from colleagues who may share a machine.

Key Highlights
  • The CISA directive covers DeepSeek's official apps and all third-party services using its API; federal civilian agencies must remove them by the specified deadline.
  • The rationale is that DeepSeek servers in China may be subject to laws requiring companies to share user data with the government, posing national security risk.
  • The move continues U.S. systematic scrutiny of Chinese tech companies, with DeepSeek becoming the latest target after TikTok.
Industry Insight

Agencies serving government clients or regulated industries should audit their AI stack to confirm vendor compliance with client data governance policies.

Read our full write-up

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) issued a directive on October 20, 2026, ordering all federal civilian agencies to cease purchasing DeepSeek technology and to remove existing DeepSeek software and services from their IT systems. The directive extends to third-party products and services built on DeepSeek's underlying technology, broadening the scope beyond the company's own applications. Agencies with specific operational needs may apply for a waiver.

The primary stated justification is national security. A September 2026 CISA report accused DeepSeek, alongside Alibaba, Moonshot AI, and three other Chinese AI firms, of large-scale model distillation — systematically extracting knowledge from American AI systems, including those of OpenAI and Anthropic, without authorization, to train their own models. The DHS directive further cites China's domestic legal framework, under which companies operating in China are obligated to cooperate with government information requests, meaning user data could theoretically be accessed by Chinese authorities.

The regulatory timeline reveals an escalating posture: a December 2025 law had already restricted DeepSeek from intelligence and national security systems; the October 2026 directive expands the prohibition to all federal civilian agencies. China's Foreign Ministry characterized the action as 'overstretching the concept of national security and politicizing technology and trade issues,' pledging to 'resolutely safeguard the legitimate rights of Chinese enterprises.'

DeepSeek built its competitive position on low-cost, open-weight models that offer a significant price differential against proprietary alternatives from OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic. China is separately reported to be weighing export controls on 23 AI-related technologies, including chip designs and advanced AI models, as a potential countermeasure. The federal directive is widely expected to accelerate the systemic decoupling between U.S. government systems and Chinese AI technology.

  • Both model families come bundled with Nvidia NIM microservice packaging to ease enterprise IT integration.
Industry Insight

Agencies can deploy open-weight agent models on-premise or on client devices, reducing cloud API dependency and running costs.

Read our full write-up

Nvidia released three open-source models under the Nemotron 3.5 series on October 19, 2026, targeting two distinct deployment contexts: enterprise agentic workflows and local AI PC inference. The flagship Nemotron 3.5 Super VL carries 120 billion parameters and is designed for enterprise-grade multimodal agent tasks, with named vertical applications including document intelligence, financial accounting, scientific experimentation, medical insurance claims processing, and security attack resilience. It is available immediately.

The Bolt sub-series addresses local compute with two models. The Bolt 74B-A9B has 74 billion total parameters but uses a sparse activation architecture, engaging only approximately 9 billion parameters per inference pass — substantially reducing compute load while preserving high capability for multi-step reasoning, tool calling, and web browsing on local hardware. It is also available immediately. The smaller Bolt E4B, at roughly 4 billion parameters, targets lightweight conversational agents and is scheduled for release later in October 2026. Both Bolt models are optimized for Nvidia RTX Spark-powered AI PCs and DGX Spark workstations, with Microsoft and Dell named as hardware partners.

Nvidia claims Super VL outperforms Anthropic Opus 5, OpenAI GPT 6.1 Sol, Luna, and Astra on post-trained agentic task benchmarks — though no independent third-party verification has been cited. The launch carries clear strategic subtext. As frontier AI labs increasingly diversify their hardware deployments to non-Nvidia chips such as AWS Trainium and Cerebras, Nvidia's near-monopoly position in AI inference hardware faces growing pressure. By releasing open-weight enterprise models, Nvidia reinforces its position within the inference software ecosystem while emphasizing that enterprises can post-train Super VL on their own data, deploy it on their own infrastructure, and retain full ownership of what they build. No pricing information was disclosed for any of the three models.

Google Open-Sources TorchTPU So AI Apps Can Migrate From Nvidia GPUs to TPUs Without Code Changes

Key Highlights
  • TorchTPU lets PyTorch AI apps run on Google Cloud TPUs with config-level changes, eliminating the need to rewrite model code.
  • Google and Amazon's parallel moves aim to free developers from mandatory Nvidia CUDA adoption — directly challenging Nvidia's ecosystem dominance.
  • More compute options should bring competitive cloud AI pricing, reducing long-term costs for agencies running high-volume AI workloads.
Industry Insight

Growing compute competition expands agency bargaining power on cloud AI services and could ease GPU subscription cost pressure.

Read our full write-up

Google open-sourced TorchTPU on October 20, 2026 — a PyTorch variant optimized for its Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) — enabling existing AI applications to migrate from Nvidia GPUs to Google TPUs through a simple configuration change, with no application rewriting required. The move directly parallels Amazon's TorchNeuron, which applies the same principle to AWS Trainium chips, marking a coordinated effort by two major cloud providers to systematically loosen Nvidia's long-standing grip on AI developers at the PyTorch ecosystem level.

PyTorch currently powers approximately 85% of AI applications worldwide. Originally built in 2016 with deep coupling to Nvidia's CUDA architecture, and transferred to the Linux Foundation in 2022, the framework has historically made Nvidia GPUs the path of least resistance for most AI workloads. TorchTPU has been verified for compatibility with Meta's PyTorch, Hugging Face, vLLM (from Inferact), and SGLang (from RadixArk), covering the major training and inference toolchains. Google also updated LiteRT simultaneously, improving its support for PyTorch applications running on phones, laptops, and embedded devices.

To reduce migration friction further, Google released a companion open-source tool on GitHub called the TPU Agent Suite — an agent-assisted toolkit that automatically identifies migration blockers in existing code and helps developers resolve errors, compressing the practical switching cost. Google frames TorchTPU as a foundational step toward making PyTorch 'more open to any hardware by default,' rather than positioning it narrowly as a Google hardware promotion.

Competitively, Amazon's TorchNeuron has contributed directly to the upstream PyTorch repository to decouple Nvidia-specific dependencies, while Google's approach adds a parallel compatibility layer. Together, they create a two-sided pressure on Nvidia: once migration costs approach zero, the price-performance advantages of TPUs and Trainium become far easier to translate into real market share. Nvidia's moat is shifting from ecosystem lock-in toward pure hardware performance competition.

Apple's WebKit Team Publicly Explains Why It Blocked LiveRamp and Other Identity Resolution Providers

Since Yesterday

On Oct 13 we covered Apple iOS 27 blocking ad trackers with eCPM dropping over 10% in the first week; Oct 14 reported the block extending to The Trade Desk. Today Apple's WebKit team publicly named LiveRamp, ID5, and UID 2.0 as blocked providers — and on the same day, the FTC approved Publicis' $2.2B LiveRamp acquisition with five-year neutrality conditions, adding a new layer of complexity to the identity resolution landscape.

Key Highlights
  • Apple WebKit publicly named LiveRamp, ID5, and UID 2.0 as providers that use first-party storage techniques to circumvent Safari's cross-site tracking protections.
  • The industry questioned Apple's dual role: blocking competitor tracking technologies while aggressively growing its own ad network through Apple Search Ads and App Store ads.
  • Agency options for targeting Safari users are narrowing; first-party data, contextual targeting, and Apple-native ad tools are the emerging response.
Industry Insight

Agencies using third-party identity tools to target Safari users need to reassess alternatives and first-party data deployment strategies.

Pattern Watch

Identity resolution is caught between two forces: Apple blocking from the technical side, the FTC conditionally approving Publicis acquiring LiveRamp from the regulatory side. Independent identity providers face a narrowing runway; first-party data is rapidly increasing in agency negotiating value.

Read our full write-up

Apple's WebKit team published a technical explanation for Safari's blocklist, covering companies including LiveRamp, ID5, The Trade Desk's UID 2.0, Permutive, and Audigent. The blocks apply across iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Vision Pro upon software updates. WebKit's stated rationale centers on email addresses having replaced third-party cookies as the primary cross-site tracking mechanism: when users enter emails on websites, third-party firms hash them into persistent identifiers that survive device switches and logouts, accumulating into detailed identity graphs over time. Safari counters this by blocking entire domains of targeted firms rather than filtering individual requests, closing off technical workarounds.

The policy has drawn criticism on transparency grounds. Apple named no companies in its public post and disclosed no criteria for blocklist inclusion or removal. At least one confirmed accidental block occurred — The Trade Desk's ad server was swept in and later restored. An ad-tech executive at a publisher noted that Apple "controls the operating system, the browser engine and increasingly the technical infrastructure," framing the blocklist as a form of market-access control rather than pure privacy enforcement.

These concerns are amplified by Apple's own expanding advertising business. Apple Ads currently has 86 open roles, including positions for Maps advertising expansion, a director of ads algorithms, and AI/ML product managers. An internal team called "Signals Intelligence" uses large language models for ad labeling and ranking. Apple's app install advertising market share surged from 17% to 58% following the ATT rollout that cost Meta roughly US$10 billion in 2022, echoing the 2017 ITP launch that severely damaged Criteo.

New CEO John Ternus, who succeeded Tim Cook last month, has signaled continued services revenue growth. Critics argue that Apple simultaneously dismantles competitors' tracking infrastructure on privacy grounds while building its own first-party ad ecosystem — a structural tension now attracting closer regulatory scrutiny of ad-tech consolidation.

FTC Approves Publicis' $2.2B LiveRamp Acquisition With Five-Year Neutrality Conditions

Key Highlights
  • The FTC mandated five-year neutral and nondiscriminatory LiveRamp access for all competitors, including non-Publicis agencies.
  • Publicis must maintain firewalls preventing access to rival clients' data through LiveRamp, blocking unfair competitive advantage.
  • The deal gives Publicis dual control over agency services and core identity infrastructure; the industry is watching what happens when the five-year conditions expire.
Industry Insight

Agencies relying on LiveRamp should note that Publicis ownership introduces uncertainty on long-term pricing and service priority; the five-year neutrality clause provides a buffer period.

Read our full write-up

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission approved Publicis Groupe's US$2.2 billion acquisition of LiveRamp Holdings on October 20, 2026, subject to a binding set of neutrality conditions effective for five years. Under the FTC order, Publicis must provide "neutral and nondiscriminatory access" to all LiveRamp products and services, ensuring competing advertising agencies retain equal access to the platform's data connectivity capabilities.

The order also requires Publicis to erect firewalls preventing its teams from accessing competitor client data that flows through LiveRamp's infrastructure. The intent is to block Publicis from leveraging LiveRamp's position as a central identity-resolution hub to extract audience intelligence or campaign strategy data belonging to rival agencies. David Shaw, principal deputy director of the FTC Bureau of Competition, stated the order will ensure "Publicis does not gain an unfair advantage over its competitors" in the digital advertising ecosystem.

LiveRamp operates as one of the world's largest data connectivity platforms, enabling secure identity matching between advertisers, media platforms, and data providers. Its infrastructure underpins audience targeting and attribution workflows across much of the third-party advertising ecosystem. Publicis sought to acquire LiveRamp to vertically integrate data infrastructure into its marketing services offering; the FTC's neutrality mandate directly targets the potential foreclosure effects of that vertical integration.

The ruling reflects the FTC's escalating scrutiny of ad-tech consolidation. Against a backdrop of Apple tightening Safari blocklists and Google repeatedly delaying third-party cookie deprecation, LiveRamp's identity resolution services carry heightened strategic value — making the five-year neutrality window a closely watched industry constraint.
