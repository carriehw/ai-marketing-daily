OpenAI's Revenue Is $20 Billion Below Earlier Projections

Key Highlights
  • OpenAI's annualised revenue is approximately $50 billion, some $20 billion below the widely cited $70 billion figure.
  • The gap stems from how cloud-partner revenue is booked, not misreporting, but affects valuation and pushes the IPO to early 2027.
  • Agencies with OpenAI-dependent tools should evaluate multi-vendor hedging as commercial pressure intensifies.
Industry Insight

Revenue shortfall may pressure OpenAI to accelerate monetisation, potentially triggering API pricing or feature-gating changes for enterprise customers.

Read our full write-up

The $20 billion discrepancy in OpenAI's reported revenue is fundamentally an accounting methodology dispute rather than a case of financial misrepresentation. According to the Financial Times investigation, the widely circulated $70 billion annualized revenue figure originated from investors who attempted to create an apples-to-apples comparison with Anthropic's reported metrics. Anthropic includes revenue flowing through cloud distribution partners such as Amazon AWS and Google Cloud in its annualized run rate calculations, while OpenAI applies a more conservative recognition approach that counts only direct bookings. This structural difference made the two figures inherently incomparable, and the systematic overestimation went unchallenged in investor circles for months.

The nearly $50 billion annualized figure, while still remarkable for a company less than a decade old, must be understood alongside OpenAI's cost structure. Leaked 2025 financials showed approximately $13 billion in total revenue against spending that exceeded that figure, leaving the company in a net loss position. The record-breaking $122 billion fundraising round completed in March 2026 provides a substantial runway, yet it also intensifies market scrutiny over the path to profitability and the timeline for a sustainable business model.

The IPO delay to early 2027 reflects ongoing complexity in OpenAI's transition from its original nonprofit governance structure to a fully commercial entity. The legal and financial engineering required for this restructuring is not yet complete, and public markets will demand greater disclosure standards than what private investors have accepted. For marketing agencies in Hong Kong and Taiwan that have built workflows around OpenAI's API stack, the core strategic implication is vendor risk diversification. When a primary AI supplier's financial transparency is questioned, the operational continuity of API-dependent services becomes a legitimate business concern — multi-vendor architecture is no longer merely a technical preference but a business continuity necessity.

The contrast with Anthropic's $6.5 billion annualized run rate (as of July) underscores how differently the two companies have positioned their distribution strategies. Anthropic's cloud-partner-inclusive accounting creates a narrative advantage in showcasing growth velocity, even as its absolute scale remains roughly one-eighth of OpenAI's. Agencies evaluating AI vendor selection should develop internal frameworks for parsing the accounting differences between competitor financial claims — treating publicly stated revenue figures as starting points for due diligence rather than definitive benchmarks.

Industry Insight

Agencies running Claude-powered content pipelines touching political topics, social media publishing, or automation must audit for compliance before the US election blackout on Oct 27.

Read our full write-up

The most substantively significant element of Anthropic's policy revision is the consolidation of previously scattered rules into a unified 'Deceptive Campaigns' section. Previously, prohibitions on fake accounts, fabricated media outlets, and coordinated influence operations existed in separate policy clauses, creating interpretive gaps that sophisticated actors could exploit. By grouping both political and commercial deception under one enforceable framework, Anthropic has raised the compliance bar for operators building Claude-powered automation tools — particularly those deploying mass social media posting scripts or brand reputation management systems that rely on accounts with unclear identity disclosure.

The adjustment to the elections section surprised some observers because it represents a deliberate narrowing rather than expansion of restriction. The blanket ban on personalized voter targeting and campaign mobilization has been removed. Anthropic's stated rationale is that the original broad prohibition was inadvertently blocking legitimate civic applications: multilingual voter information services, nonpartisan voting guides, and civic education tools were being caught in the same net as malicious interference campaigns. The revised language focuses specifically on voter deception and election process disruption, taking effect November 12, 2026.

For agencies building automated content tools or social media management services on Claude's API, the central compliance question concerns the boundary between legitimate automation and influence operations. Anthropic does not provide quantitative thresholds. The governing test appears to be whether the activity is designed to deceive the public about the true distribution of opinion on a topic. In practical terms, this requires that automated posting tools built on Claude maintain transparent account identities, operate within platform-native behavioral norms, and retain auditable records of genuine user authorization for each action taken.

The new hardware control requirements — mandating that operators connected to physical devices maintain human override capability and ensure safe-state fallback on disconnection — have limited direct relevance to marketing agencies today. Their significance lies in establishing a clear regulatory expectation: Anthropic is drawing a firm line around the scope of AI autonomous action. For agencies evaluating AI integration into programmatic media buying or automated campaign optimization systems, this signals that compliance frameworks for AI-assisted decision-making will become increasingly specific and enforceable as the technology matures.

Anthropic Launches Cyber Mission: CIDP for Critical Infrastructure, Free OSS Scanner

Since Yesterday

On Oct 7 we covered Anthropic's three-tier cybersecurity verification program (CIDP), with partners having found 129,000+ software vulnerabilities. Today Anthropic formally elevated this into a full 'Cyber Mission' framework — adding the free OSS Scanner on top of the verification program to complete its public cyber-defence layer.

Key Highlights
  • OSS Scanner is free for anyone to use, enabling agencies to audit open-source dependencies for security vulnerabilities at no cost.
  • CIDP continues expanding its partner network to cover AI-powered defence of power, water, and transport critical infrastructure.
  • Anthropic projects AI will broadly 'favor defense' over offense within two years, but acknowledges near-term risks remain elevated.
Industry Insight

Agencies can now use OSS Scanner for free to audit open-source tools and third-party integrations in their martech stacks, reducing supply-chain risk at no cost.

Read our full write-up

Anthropic's Cyber Mission, launched October 8, 2026, addresses a structural asymmetry in cybersecurity: AI lowers the cost of both attack and defense, but defensive adoption has historically lagged. The Critical Infrastructure Defense Program (CIDP) tackles this head-on by embedding frontier Claude models and on-site Anthropic engineers directly within operational technology (OT) environments — power grids, water systems, transportation networks, and manufacturing facilities — that cannot be taken offline for patching and often run decades-old proprietary equipment. The founding partner cohort of 11 organizations spans consulting (Accenture, Booz Allen, Deloitte, PwC), pure-play cybersecurity (CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike), OT/ICS specialists (Dragos, Nozomi Networks, Insane Cyber), and industrial equipment manufacturers (Hitachi, Rockwell Automation). Since June 2026, a predecessor program has already served more than half of all US state and local governments, delivering code scanning, patch management, incident response, and red-team exercises.

The OSS Scanner is modeled after Google's OSS-Fuzz and offers free, opt-in periodic vulnerability scanning for open-source projects. Each report is model-generated without human review — enabling faster delivery — and includes a proof-of-concept exploit, root-cause explanation, and suggested fix where available. Anthropic targets a true-positive rate above 90%. High-triage-capacity maintainers can receive raw bulk findings; others go through a coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD) pipeline with human verification. The service grew out of Project Glasswing, which identified a chronic problem: months often passed between vulnerability discovery and patching. The Defender Advantage Fund (0xDAF), launched in August 2026, keeps the scanner free and supports organizations including the Python Software Foundation, OpenSSF, and the Apache Software Foundation.

A third track — the Cyber Verification Program (CVP), merged with Glasswing this week — widens access to Claude's full cybersecurity capabilities for security professionals, operators, and maintainers of any organization size, with relaxed blocking classifiers to support legitimate penetration testing and reverse engineering. For marketing technology teams, the practical implication is direct: ad-tech stacks increasingly depend on open-source libraries, making supply-chain attacks a genuine vector. OSS Scanner now offers a zero-cost way to audit those dependencies. Anthropic's two-year forecast predicts AI will ultimately favor defenders — lower-friction pre-ship bug detection, secure-by-default code generation, and active AI-assisted monitoring — while acknowledging a near-term window where offensive tooling may retain the edge as defensive adoption catches up.

The architecture centers on a task inbox that surfaces the agent's reasoning chain, subagent delegation calls, skill-loading events, and live progress tracking — giving operators full visibility without requiring manual supervision. Model selection is automated by default, routing each subtask to the optimal available model, but enterprises can override to specify Anthropic Claude or, in future updates, open-source or on-premise models. Google's decision to launch business-first — explicitly citing the need to resolve security, scale, and performance challenges before a consumer rollout — signals a deliberate maturation strategy.
