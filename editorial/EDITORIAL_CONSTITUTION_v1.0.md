# Carrie Hui AI Marketing Daily — Editorial Constitution v1.0

**Status:** Adopted editorial policy · **Effective date:** 2026-10-26 · **Owner:** Carrie Hui  
**Applies to:** Website editions, archived articles, summaries, bilingual headlines, EDM, copy-for-team briefs, and derived social posts.  
**Timezone / edition date:** Asia/Hong_Kong (HKT; UTC+08:00).

## 1. Mission

**Accuracy earns trust. Relevance earns attention. Actionable intelligence earns the share.**

AI Marketing Daily is an editorial intelligence briefing **for marketers, agency professionals, brand managers and business decision-makers**. It explains verified developments in artificial intelligence, advertising platforms, MarTech, creative production, measurement and brand practice, and their practical implications for marketing decisions.

**Editorial promise:** A reader can identify what actually changed, how reliable the claim is, why a marketer should care, and a proportionate next step. A daily issue is a curated decision brief, **not a complete feed of everything published about AI**.

## 2. Non-negotiable independence and accuracy

1. **Evidence before writing.** The existing website or past editions are discovery inputs, not proof. Open and evaluate the original source underlying every material claim.
2. **No invented facts.** Do not invent facts, figures, interviews, quotes, research samples, dates, market availability, prices, capabilities, model names, revenue or tested outcomes.
3. **Separate four layers:** (a) verified facts, (b) attributed claims, (c) reasoned marketer implications, (d) uncertain possibilities. Do not turn (b–d) into (a).
4. **Preserve status:** distinguish *announced / planned / limited pilot / beta / available to all / independently evaluated*. Preserve country, audience, tier, device and timeline limitations.
5. **Preserve measurement context:** keep numerator/denominator, sample, geography, timeframe, currency, annualised vs realised revenue, forecast vs historical measurement, and methodological caveats wherever material.
6. **Never rely on a URL alone.** A matching-looking headline, a functioning URL, or an article cited by another article is not sufficient fact-checking.
7. **No synthetic first-person experience.** Do not imply Carrie personally tested a product, interviewed a source, attended an event or saw campaign outcomes unless verified.
8. **Do not equate speed with quality.** It is acceptable to publish fewer stories, delay an issue, or clearly mark an incomplete edition rather than publish unsupported claims.
9. **No silent corrections.** Material factual changes must leave a dated correction note and a traceable repository revision.
10. **No auto-publication of unresolved high-risk material.** High-risk material is held for review or excluded.

## 3. Source and evidence rules

**Source classes are a guide to corroboration, not automatic truth scores.**

- **A · Primary:** first-party product documentation and changelogs, official company statements, regulator/court/stock-exchange filings, original study and methods, verifiable direct interview.
- **B · Independently reported:** established news reporting with original interviews, documents or direct confirmation. Record attribution and anonymous-source limitations.
- **C · Secondary / analysis:** technology and trade press, expert commentary, republished articles. Trace material claims to the primary material where feasible.
- **D · Unverified:** leaks, unsourced social claims, screenshots without provenance, anonymous speculation. Do not publish as verified news. Report as a clearly attributed developing claim only where public importance warrants and an editor approves.

**Material fact gate** — for each selected story:
- Identify **canonical original URL**, **actual event date**, **original publication/update date** and **verified-at timestamp**.
- Capture at least **one supporting evidence locator** for each pivotal claim: original source URL plus short quote/excerpt, exact table/section, or page reference. **A first-party company statement is evidence of what that company says, not proof of independent performance.**
- High-impact contested facts, exceptional numbers, financial/regulatory assertions and surprising product claims require **independent corroboration where available**; otherwise qualify explicitly or hold.
- If behind a paywall or otherwise inaccessible, record that limitation. Do not infer undisclosed details.
- Check links resolve to the **relevant article**, not merely an active home page.
- Dates: explicitly differentiate **event date**, **source publication date** and **edition date**. Older developments may be carried when relevant, marked accurately.

### Claim labels (internal)

- `verified`: original material inspected; wording justified.
- `attributed`: claim reliably sourced to a named party but not independently established.
- `uncertain`: reported possibility, limited evidence or unresolved interpretation; normally excluded or qualified.
- `rejected`: incorrect, unsupported or contradictory — must not publish as fact.

### Immediate hold / block

Block or remove a claim where the URL is fabricated/mismatched; original facts do not support the headline; numbers or dates contradict evidence; translation materially changes the meaning; an unconfirmed claim is written as confirmed; a legal, financial, privacy or brand-safety finding is overstated; or evidence is absent for a pivotal point.

## 4. Selection and ranking

The **Editorial Relevance Score** ranks **already fact-checked** candidates (maximum 100):

| Dimension | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Direct relevance to marketers' work | 30 |
| Business and performance impact | 25 |
| Actionability for a marketer or team | 20 |
| Hong Kong / Greater China / APAC relevance | 15 |
| Novelty and genuine new development | 10 |

**Editorial priority:** `80–100` Top Story candidate; `65–79` Main Brief candidate; `50–64` Radar or context; `0–49` usually omit. Human editorial judgement and source uncertainty can **downgrade** any item. High scores **never override verification gates**.

Choose **up to three** lead stories, a compact main brief of verified developments, and optional Radar headlines. **Daily editorial service standard: at least 10 individually source-verified, genuinely relevant full-news items for automatic publication; target 12–18.** Three are highlighted as Top Stories, rather than being the whole issue. Short Radar items do not count toward the ten unless they meet the full story and evidence standard. When fewer than ten qualify, expand credible discovery; if still short, hold automatic publication and report the gap for Publisher review. Never fill the quota with duplicate or unsupported items. An empty category is preferable to filler. Distinguish genuine updates of an ongoing story from simple duplicate coverage; link back and state what materially changed.

For action labels, separate:
- **Impact:** High / Medium / Low.
- **Recommended response:** Act Now / Test / Monitor / Inform.
Impact measures potential significance; response indicates the editorially warranted next action.

## 5. Standard story anatomy

**Required reader-facing structure:**
1. **Headline:** precise, useful, non-sensational, with accurate tense/status. No unsupported absolute claims.
2. **What happened:** 1–3 factual sentences, with key limits and concrete specifics.
3. **Why marketers should care:** a concise **reasoned implication**, not a new fact claim.
4. **Recommended action:** something proportionate and realistically testable or reviewable. Write *No immediate action* when appropriate.
5. **What remains uncertain:** only if important; do not invent caveats.
6. **Source and date:** transparent original sources, actual date of source/event; mark paywall.

**Optional:** Since Yesterday (genuine event progression only), Pattern Watch (explicitly forward-looking, evidence/logic shown), and concise highlights.

**Forbidden:** vague "AI is changing everything" filler, forced agency upsell, causation inferred from correlation, market impact claimed without data, unlabelled predictions, invented practical tests.

## 6. Editorial language and accessibility

- Write professional and idiomatic **Traditional Chinese for Hong Kong**, not machine-translation phrasing. Prefer polished written Chinese in the website. Avoid the connective 「但／但係／不過」 in authored copy as an editorial style preference; never alter quotations or factual substance to satisfy style.
- Independently edit **English**, preserving identical numbers, status, names, product versions, source dates and limitations.
- Chinese and English are two editorial versions of the **same facts**, not different claims. Do not mask missing English translations with unnoticed Chinese fallback.
- Expand unfamiliar terms on first reference where useful. No jargon for its own sake.
- Quote sparingly, attribute responsibly, link to original reporting. Avoid reproducing full copyrighted articles or paywalled substance.
- Mobile-first readability, clear heading hierarchy, meaningful link labels, alt text, sufficient colour contrast.
- Disclose meaningful sponsorship or conflicts. Paid placement must never masquerade as editorial selection.

## 7. Correction and transparency

**Correction labels:** `Minor edit` (typography); `Correction` (material factual fix); `Update` (new development, previous version was accurate); `Retraction` (story no longer supportable).

Material changes require: edition date, article identifier, original issue, corrected wording, UTC/HKT change timestamp, new evidence URL(s), GitHub commit, and a short reader-facing note. Do not silently rewrite archives. For an unsupported news item, retract/annotate or remove while leaving an accessible correction trail.

Maintain a public **Methodology & Corrections** section eventually; internal evidence logs may remain in the repository. Never place private credentials or confidential client records in publicly hosted assets.

## 8. Who is accountable

| Role | Accountability |
| --- | --- |
| Researcher / AI assistant | Gather candidates, inspect provenance, draft evidence records |
| Independent fact checker / second-pass AI reviewer | Re-examine pivotal claims against sources; challenge unsupported language |
| Senior editor & marketer | Score, prioritise, refine headline and marketer implications; approve risk posture |
| Publishing operator | Build and validate website / EDM / archive; verify live edition, report failure |
| Publisher, Carrie Hui | Editorial ownership, overrides and approval of sensitive/exceptional pieces |

Roles may be implemented by tools/agents only when an actual separate check is performed. **Multiple labels or repeated prompts are not independent verification.** For contested, high-consequence or unverifiable stories, require explicit human review.

## 9. Release standard and service expectations

1. The issue date is **the actual HKT edition date**, never an invented publication date.
2. Every published material assertion is traceable to an evidence record; unsupported assertions are **blocked**.
3. Source URL, date, claims, language parity, duplication, article integrity, archive/EDM consistency and rendering are checked **before** publish.
4. On GitHub Pages, a successful code commit or HTTP 200 does **not** prove that the new issue is live; independently verify the actual live date/content and archive.
5. Publication failure must be **reported as failure**, with a clear blocker and the last confirmed issue date. Never claim success when only a draft or commit exists.
6. On a day with insufficient reliable news or unavailable access: explicitly say **No verified issue published** rather than fill with speculation.
7. Monitor editorial outcomes: relevant-story clicks, meaningful outbound reads, copy/share intent, return visits and corrections. Never equate clicks or clicks on a copy button with verified forwards.

## 10. Change control

The Constitution defines the **editorial standard**; the Daily SOP implements it. Version both documents in GitHub. A policy change needs a dated commit, rationale and explicit acknowledgment of altered gates. Automation prompts must read and follow **the current version on the repository**, not rely on a past-chat instruction.

### 2026-10-28 amendment — 60-point direct selection rule

- After full candidate discovery, de-duplication and initial editorial triage, place every candidate with a **provisional editorial priority score >=60/100** directly into the **selected-for-verification editorial list**; candidates below 60 are normally excluded (an explicit, documented Publisher exception is required to include one).
- This is an automatic **selection for editorial processing**, not automatic fact approval or publication. A 60+ item that repeats prior coverage without material new information, has a misleading event date, lacks original evidence, or fails independent review must be marked `HELD/REJECTED` with reason; it must never bypass any release gate.
- Assess material follow-ups on incremental verified developments, not mere related commentary. Where 60+ eligible items exceed the desired daily volume, process the highest-value items first, retain the others as qualified reserves; do not silently discard or translate everything prematurely.
- First lock the eligible editorial list and source verification; finalize Traditional Chinese, Simplified Chinese and English only for verified editorial-approved items. Existing draft translations may be reused after approval.
- Maintain the **>=10 independently verified and approved full-story publication floor**, evidence, language parity, archive, newsletter, build/CI and live verification. Score does not count as verification. If fewer than ten pass, hold the issue.
- Record each candidate's score, selected/held/rejected state and brief reason in the daily draft for auditability.
